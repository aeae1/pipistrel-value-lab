# Pipistrel Virus + Sinus: purchase-price market research and valuation methodology

**Research snapshot:** October 15, 2026  
**Scope:** Used and historical Pipistrel **Virus** and **Sinus** only; worldwide advertisement evidence; approximate acquisition costs in Texas.  
**Out of scope:** Panthera, Alpha, Velis, Taurus, ownership economics, financing, annual insurance and recurring flight expenses.  
**Verification standard:** A source saying a listing disappeared or was “sold” is **not** accepted as proof of an achieved sale price.

## Executive interpretation

Virus and Sinus are exceptionally poor candidates for a one-number price guide. Even within the same calendar year, three aircraft can have very different asking prices due to the *approved aircraft category*, engine specification, the gearbox/propeller combination, avionics, damage/maintenance records and especially the difference between **US light-sport** and **European ultralight** markets. The history of engine and rescue-system service is also important: a plane can have few hours and very little remaining calendar life before a substantial event.

We retained **92** source-linked records: **46** model-eligible, individually priced references and **46** price-history, factory/new-price, archive, ambiguous, or otherwise excluded contextual references. The explicitly non-airworthy $49,000 Phoenix salvage Virus is deliberately removed from the dataset and every chart; it is not a comparison for a flying aircraft. **31** of the excluded observations are curated fragments from an underlying **43-ad** Aero-Offers Sinus history collection; many lack reliable manufacturing year or are repeat ads. The background research library has **35** links. These counts are for the October 15 snapshot, not live inventories or independently completed transactions.

The output deliberately consists of a modeled *typical ask*, a separate hypothetical *negotiated midpoint*, and a **broad purchase-price interval**. It does **not** assert that the midpoint is an actual fair-value sale result. Known non-airworthy, salvage, parts-only or restoration-project airframes are completely outside this research and price database. Ordinary for-sale aircraft are not independently inspected; airworthiness must still be confirmed. A custom expert prebuy, serial verification, airworthiness/registration check, and seller logbook review are necessary before any purchase offer.

| Original long-wing Virus | Older efficient, longer-wing Virus distinct from SW | Prop, engine, UL registration, assembly/servicing and age |
| Virus SW | SW80 / SW100 / 100iS / SW600D | 912 ULS carbureted vs **912 iS injected**, options and approved MTOW |

| SW121A Explorer | Later certified derivative | Production year, differences between certified and experimentally registered examples |
| Classic Sinus | Long-wing motorglider with Rotax 912, typically 80hp | Towing equipment, glide/wing condition, feathering prop, service history |
| Sinus Flex / Max Flex | Variable wingtip length/configuration | Inclusion of BOTH tip sets, US LSA vs EU UL certification and avionics |
| Recent Sinus Max | Modern models marketed as Sinus Max | Recent new-price frame, scarce secondhand data, wingtip sets and panel |

**Mandatory warning:** The standard Rotax 912 S3 fitted on SW121/Explorer is **carbureted**, whereas **912 iS** denotes injection. An aircraft described as “Virus SW121 iS” needs an exact model/serial and approval review instead of assuming a factory-legal swap. The calculator shows a warning for this or a non-standard injected Sinus; it does not authorize a modification. ## 3. Engine valuation: hours AND calendar years

The correct remaining time for a stated hour-based and calendar-based TBO is constrained by **both**. The app displays both raw limits and uses the smaller *fraction* of the two horizons to estimate engine age pressure:

```
hours_remaining = applicable_TBO_hours − engine_hours_since_new_or_overhaul
years_remaining = overhaul_or_installation_year + TBO_calendar_years − 2026
fraction_remaining = clamp(min(hours_remaining / TBO_hours,
                               years_remaining / TBO_calendar_years), 0, 1)
```

Example: a stated 2,000-hour, 15-year engine installed in 2012, at 400 hours in 2026, has 1,600 nominal hours left but only **one calendar year** before the 2027 calendar deadline. Treating that engine as 80% unused for resale purposes would miss the relevant near-term service event. If calendar expiration occurs, the tool warns; whether overhaul is a regulatory requirement or manufacturer recommendation depends on authority, exact certificate and documentation. The tool does **not** determine legal operating permission.

Some early 912UL/ULS serials had limits of 1,000/1,200/1,500 hours; later units may reach 2,000 hours after specified changes or on later serials, plus varying calendar intervals. Specific applicable Rotax edition, service bulletins, engine serial and overhaul documentation take precedence over defaults. Sources: [Rotax 912 iS maintenance documentation](https://www.rotax-owner.com/manuals/MML_912iSeries_Ed2_R0.pdf) and [Rotax 912-series maintenance/TBO schedule](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1960422/Brp-Rotax-912-Series.html?page=44).

### What the engine-age adjustment actually costs in the model

The site deliberately uses a fraction of hypothetical overhaul/replacement expenditure to estimate what a buyer would subtract from a typical asking price. It uses baseline repair-cost hypotheses of **$24,500 (non-turbo 912)**, **$29,000 (912iS)** and **$30,000 (914)**, multiplies them by **0.49** and a non-linear TBO-used fraction, and **compares the selected engine's burden to a same-year benchmark engine**. It does *not* assume a buyer can buy a new engine or obtain an overhaul for only 49% of the hypothetical cost. Replacement vs overhaul actual quotations are model- and shop-dependent. The larger purchase valuation adjustment is a judgment, not direct market-derived regression. Treat it as adjustable sensitivity rather than a manufacturer quoted price.

## 4. Parachutes and other near-term costs

A rescue system has at least two time dimensions: **fabric/canopy/system inspection or repack** and **rocket/pyrotechnic expiry**. They may not share a date. The app asks for parachute model presence, last repack year, a configurable interval and rocket expiry year. Typical Galaxy GRS/BRS service intervals can be around **six years**, but the correct document from the maker, installation serial and jurisdiction must control. Confirm access to approved packing shops and freight restrictions. Sources: [Galaxy GRS repack guidance](https://www.galaxysky.cz/parachute-rescue-system-repack-and-revision-s128-en) and [BRS rebuild service](https://brsaerospace.com/rebuild/).

The current **resale penalties are editorial priors**, not repack-shop quotations:

| Known remaining repack horizon | Modeled value reduction vs a recently serviced system |
|---|---:|
| Overdue/now due | roughly $3,200 |
| Within 2 years | roughly $2,000 |
| Within 4 years | roughly $1,100 |
| Within 6 years | roughly $250 |
| Beyond 6 years | $0 |
| Rocket due/expired | an additional ~$1,800 |
| Rocket within 2 years | an additional ~$700 |

These are illustrative *resale* impacts; actual invoices and downtime can be higher or lower. Other field-based pressure used in the purchase model: prop due ~$3,400, five-year Rotax rubber overdue ~$2,500, gearbox work overdue ~$3,000, and smaller deductions for unknown or near-due service status. Missing documentation also widens the estimated interval. An absent rescue system has a separate roughly **−$6,200** resale-feature assumption; it is *not* double charged as an overdue repack.

## 5. Avionics, autopilot and optional equipment value priors

How much does a $15k avionics install add to *resale*? Not necessarily $15k. The following are **modeling hypotheses for incremental market preference** relative to the reference Rotax 912 ULS + legacy glass + in-flight adjustable prop baseline, not individual invoice costs and not verified regression coefficients. The calculator uses those values for transparent equipment sensitivity.

| Option group | Editable scenario option | Assumed market effect before subtracting that variant's standard package |
|---|---|---:|
| Rotax engine | 912 UL 80 hp / 912 ULS 100 hp / 912 iS FI / certified 912 S3 / 914 turbo | −$7,000 / $0 / **+$14,000** / +$1,500 / +$11,000 |
| Panel | Analog / legacy glass / Dynon HDX / Garmin G3X / G1000 class | −$7,000 / $0 / **+$6,200** / **+$9,000** / +$11,500 |
| Autopilot | None / basic / commissioned two-axis | $0 / +$2,600 / **+$6,500** |
| Propeller | Fixed / ground-adjustable / in-flight-adjustable / hydraulic constant-speed | −$6,300 / −$3,700 / $0 / +$4,700 |
| GPS navigation | Basic / GNX-class / GTN650 / GTN750 | +$500 / +$3,100 / +$5,500 / +$9,000 |
| GLASS ADS-B, FLARM, dual glass | Each documented installed item | +$1,500 / +$1,350 / +$3,100 |
| Soaring/baggage options | Tow kit, both Flex wingtip sets, long-range tanks | +$3,300 / +$4,800 / +$1,750 |
| Other convenience | Oxygen, interior, brakes, heated pitot, trim, LEDs, preheat, fairings | Generally +$400 to +$2,100 each |

A factory package often **already includes** a Garmin panel or autopilot. We subtract the selected model variant's benchmark package once to avoid crediting a normal factory-installed component twice. A very high option tally should still be regarded skeptically: upgrades can be tightly correlated, obsolete, improperly installed, lacking STC/approval, or not functioning. The fitted system's paperwork matters more than a bullet-point seller list.

## 6. Price algorithm, evidence weights and interval

The exact algorithm is readable in [scripts/app.js](scripts/app.js), particularly `calculate`, `compWeight`, `comparableAdjust`, `maintenanceDelta`, and `acquisition`.

1. Start from a *disclosed* reference-year/price for each variant. These seven prior anchors are **modeling assumptions**, *not factory price sheets*.
2. Shift the prior by about **2.9% per build year**, within a cap; adjust airframe time by approximately **$11–20/hour**, with bounded influence.
3. For each usable same-family advertisement, normalize variant/reference year and any **known** time and equipment differences. Preserve unknown equipment as unknown; never infer “no parachute” from silence.
4. Discount a comp for poor identification, stale date, extremely divergent year/hours, suspicious price, known duplicate risk, and a different sales/registration market. A foreign-market comp typically carries **55% of same-market base weight**, not the same credibility as a U.S. LSA in a U.S. deal.
5. Prefer **exact-variant** comps whenever three or more exist. Compute a robust trimmed weighted median/mean instead of taking the arithmetic mean of advertisements. Blend this comparable reference with the prior according to effective matched sample size; strong matched comps contribute at most **82%**.
6. Apply condition, known repair history, uncertain logbooks and engine/repack/prop/gearbox status. A negotiated concession percentage (default **4%**) is an explicit **assumption**, not a reported sold-versus-ask discount.
7. Display a **wide heuristic interval** (generally ±17–47% around the modeled midpoint). Particularly heterogeneous Sinus Flex has a minimum ±35% band; Explorer a minimum ±30%. **This is not a measured statistical confidence interval** and does not guarantee a sale will fall inside it.

The model separates **price** confidence (scarcity of exact comparable advertisements) from **legal/useability uncertainty** (whether the registration and engine/parachute hardware are acceptable for the buyer's planned U.S. operations). A low foreign advertised ask does not make a foreign ultralight eligible for FAA aircraft registration.

### Evidence holdout audit (not a sales-price test)

A reproducible leave-one-listing-out audit was run on the **38 model-eligible observations with known airframe hours**. For each source-listed aircraft, remove that asking record, configure year/time/known equipment, retain other comps and estimate the missing asking price with **0% negotiated reduction**. Results:

| Statistic | Result |
|---|---:|
| Records included in leave-one-out | 38 |
| Median absolute error against withheld **asking** prices | **9.9%** |
| Asks within the heuristic ask-to-ask interval | **31 / 38 (81.6%)** |
| Sinus Flex subset | **6 examples**, median absolute error **13.9%** |
| Original long-wing Virus subset | **2 examples**, median absolute error **29.9%** |

The most severe residuals are **2018 European Sinus Max/Flex €69k**, **2017 U.S. Sinus Flex $69,900**, and the **$169k 2023 Explorer**. These cases are not hidden: they are explicit in [tests/backtest-results.csv](tests/backtest-results.csv), and widen the model intervals. This within-sample, retrospectively assembled corpus has correlated ads, incomplete exact specs, manually chosen priors and incomplete regulatory classifications. It **cannot** establish that a typical buyer will negotiate within 9.9% of the calculator's purchase midpoint. No verified sale prices were used, so such a claim would be unsound.

## 7. International import and Texas acquisition assumptions

The tool separately prices the hypothetical **landed purchase**, not ongoing ownership. Default logistics cost bands, which the user can edit, are:

| Seller location | Approximate transport/logistics allowance | PPI/documentation/assembly allowance |
|---|---:|---:|
| Continental USA | $900–$3,000 | $2,500 |
| Europe/UK | **$14,000–$30,000** | **$6,000** |
| Canada | $3,000–$9,000 | $4,200 |
| Other regions | $18,000–$40,000 | $7,000 |

These are exploratory **budgets**, not freight/broker offers, and vary wildly depending on wings removal, crating, insured transport, port selection, customs and FAA inspection or rigging. Check whether wing assemblies and transport trailer are included. Aircraft insurance during ocean freight and VAT export paperwork can be meaningful extras.

The currency input starts at **1 EUR = $1.16 USD** solely as a demonstration assumption; it is **not a live exchange rate**. Set it to the executed transfer rate, including any conversion spread. Some European asking prices quote VAT-inclusive values and others ex-VAT: the **VAT recovery field defaults to 0** and should only be used after written dealer/customs confirmation. Customs duty defaults to **0 as a placeholder**, not a conclusion about current temporary duties, product origin or 2026 tariffs. Any applicable special surcharge requires broker review. U.S. statutory base tariff classification may be duty-free for some civil aircraft but country-of-origin rules, current additional duties and aircraft classification matter; see [USITC Harmonized Tariff Schedule](https://hts.usitc.gov/search?query=8802.20.01).

Texas state sales/use tax on aircraft is generally **6.25%**, with local tax up to **2%** potentially relevant depending on transaction/use and statutory exceptions. The default **8.25%** is a conservative editable scenario, not a guarantee of what is owed. See [Texas Comptroller aircraft guidance](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-168.php) and [Texas aircraft tax ruling](https://star.comptroller.texas.gov/view/202001033P).

**Illustrative FI Virus example** (not a purchase recommendation): the **€120,000** D-MAFV SW100 912iS ask, converted at assumed 1.16, is **$139,200** before transport. Add hypothetical Europe freight midrange **$22,000**, acquisition paperwork/assembly **$6,000**, and a hypothetical 8.25% tax on the aircraft price **$11,484**: **$178,684** total, *assuming zero additional duty, zero VAT recovery and no extra unbudgeted costs*. Unknown parachute status and **unverified US airworthiness eligibility** are not resolved by that total. The actual deal could differ sharply.

### Foreign-aircraft legality is a separate go/no-go test

FAA aircraft **registration**, an **airworthiness certificate**, and a manufacturer's declaration/accepted airworthiness basis are separate matters. European microlight/VDS/UL paperwork is not interchangeable with U.S. S-LSA or certified standard category, nor automatically eligible for any experimental category. The exact factory serial, historic standards-compliance documents, approval basis, logbooks, import documentation and inspection route must be assessed **before** sending a nonrefundable deposit. MOSAIC regulatory updates do not themselves confer automatic certification on an arbitrary imported European UL. See [FAA importing foreign aircraft](https://www.faa.gov/licenses_certificates/aircraft_certification/aircraft_registry/import_aircraft/index.cfm) and [FAA Light Sport](https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/gen_av/light_sport).

## 8. What I would ask before valuing any exact aircraft

1. Factory invoice/serial number, manufacture year vs first flight vs certificate issue, factory variant, FAA/EASA/UL approval documents and signed aircraft declarations.
2. Full logs and receipts, ownership chain, damage/repair records, composite crack and wing-root inspection notes, hangar vs sun exposure.
3. Engine model and **serial number**, **engine hours since overhaul versus airframe TT**, last qualifying overhaul/installation date, actual eligible hour/calendar TBO and bulletins completed. Evidence of coolant/oil and five-year hose service.
4. Gearbox, clutch, exhaust and propeller type/serial, last servicing, AD/SB compliance, pitch/feather/constant-speed function and documented approval.
5. Exact GRS/BRS kit/rocket **serials**, last pack dates, manufacturer's required schedule, rocket expiration, import/shipping restrictions, feasible servicing location.
6. Garmin/Dynon/Kanardia exact display versions, firmware, IFR approval only if legally documented, autopilot servos and flight testing, ADS-B/transponder compliance, FAA database entry where relevant.
7. Factory weight & balance, payload with full tank, both wingtip sets if Flex, max takeoff weight legally approved for this serial, hidden costs of wing disassembly and crate.
8. An independent **Pipistrel-experienced prebuy** and fresh documentation photos before any deposit, plus written U.S. certification/import confirmation for overseas candidates.

## 9. Known limitations and future research direction

- **No public complete transaction-price dataset** for Virus/Sinus was found. The 4% negotiated discount is a user-adjustable assumption. Do not interpret it as measured bargaining leverage.
- Factory pricing varies by country, configuration, tax and serial. Some 2026 new-aircraft numbers online are **broker assertions**, not signed OEM quotations. Manufacturer MSRP should be obtained from a dealer for the *exact* spec before making comparisons.
- The Sinus Flex population is not homogeneous: older low-priced EU ULs and newer $168–199k U.S. LSA advertisements are not economically identical despite common naming.
- Listings with no engine or parachute description remain unknown. The UI's default is a **hypothetical aircraft configuration** and does not assert unverified equipment on any specific seller's airplane.
- Source indexes and some airframe history may conflict. We retain flags and exclude known impossible early “certified SW121” ads from model weighting.
- Asset condition, paperwork and tariffs can cause price differences greater than equipment line-item estimates. Buyers should revise options after seeing actual inspection records.
- This is a **static dated research snapshot**. It does not silently collect updates or suggest that a dated listing is still in stock.

## 16 October 2026 UI / model audit update

The 92-record source dataset and 46 eligible records are retained. The October 2026 comparison against an independently generated aircraft pricing dashboard exposed its weaknesses: fixed straight-line depreciation and ±8% nominal range produced substantial asking-price errors, and it mixed non-airworthy salvage aircraft into its reference collection. We did not merge that valuation formula or aircraft database.

Features adapted as interaction ideas: sortable market data, a categorized **resale-prior** (not factory-option-price) guide, listing-to-scenario preset loading, a change-since-last-edit indicator, and whole-dollar price displays. Source-specific fields (engine time, engine TBO, calendar TBO, Garmin/Dynon avionics, parachute repack due, propeller) are shown where documented and remain **Unknown** elsewhere. Market table USD conversions use the editable scenario FX (initially 1 EUR = $1.16) and do not pretend to be spot-correct at each historical listing date.

**Important accuracy nuance:** entering a listing preset cannot establish omitted engine serial/TBO or repack dates. The preset marks TBO documentation uncertain, repack status unknown, and displays an explicit note about hypothetical placeholders. The market table displays a claimed residual TBO but does not independently validate engine serial eligibility. Known non-airworthy/salvage/parts-only planes are absent; seller-advertised airworthiness itself remains unverified.
