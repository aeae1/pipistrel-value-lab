The raw source count is not the number of independent sale observations; the 31 curated archive entries deliberately lack year-based model weight because their build years aren't established. Some may still overlap current listings.

How the valuation is calculated

It is a transparent comparable-adjustment heuristic, not a machine-learned appraisal or fabricated regression. The small and heterogeneous market cannot support a defensible many-variable statistical model from public transactions alone.

1. Identify exact-variant comparable asking prices. 2. Deduplicate by known registration / listing identity. 3. Normalize known year, TTAF, equipment and location. 4. Weight by match quality, location/airworthiness-market mismatch, evidence completeness, and recency. 5. Blend robust comparable midpoint with disclosed variant prior. 6. Adjust for time-to-TBO, parachute/prop, history and condition. 7. Assume a negotiable discount; display a wide uncertainty interval. 8. Separately estimate freight, duty, Texas tax and inspection. No completed transaction prices were verified.

No information in an advertisement is silently assumed to mean equipment is absent. Non-airworthy aircraft, parts-only listings and repair projects are excluded from the dataset entirely. Factory-new prices and duplicate ads are excluded from used-aircraft valuation weighting. When fewer than three close references exist, the estimate is driven more heavily by explicit, editable pricing assumptions.

Technical variables that matter

  • Engine family and serial: 912UL/ULS/912S3 are carbureted; 912iS is injected. The 912ULS TBO may be 1,000/1,200/1,500/2,000 hours depending on serial and modifications; the 912iS typical schedule is 2,000 hours / 15 years. Never grant a 2,000-hour extension on a seller claim alone.
  • Calendar vs hour time remaining: value reflects both. An engine with 1,500 flight hours left but a calendar deadline already passed is not “75% remaining.”
  • Parachute: Galaxy GRS and BRS are different systems. Typical repack is six years; rockets and usable life are separately tracked.
  • Autopilot: installed and commissioned servos are not the same as a display that's merely autopilot-ready.
  • Certified vs microlight: SW121 / Explorer have a different value and regulatory proposition from Virus SW100 ultralights. A certified 912 S3 is not fuel-injected.
  • Storage & repairs: decades-old composite airplanes need thorough inspection even with very few logged flight hours.

International acquisition: what this is not

A foreign Euro-ultralight does not automatically become a registerable U.S. light-sport aircraft. Approval basis, exact production records, serial numbers, declarations of compliance, instruments, possible experimental certificate eligibility and FAA inspection matter, particularly after MOSAIC rule changes.

The import figure is intentionally a planning guesstimate, with default Europe→Texas physical logistics about $14k–$30k; add PPI and administrative fees separately. Actual quotes will depend on crate/container, disassembly, insurance, customs broker, assembly, rigging and airworthiness inspection. Excluding or refunding EU VAT must be contractually confirmed. U.S. heading 8802.20.01 has a free column-1 base duty but 2026 special tariffs may still apply; duty is left editable.

Texas aircraft sales/use tax: 6.25% state + potentially up to 2% local tax, subject to specific exemptions and credits. Get formal confirmation on the exact transaction.
